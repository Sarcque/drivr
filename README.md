# Drivr

A navigation app that shows the road the way it really looks: every lane, its markings and
arrows, and exactly which lane to be in. Built only on free and open components and OpenStreetMap
data. No API keys, no paid services.

| Piece | What | Licence |
|---|---|---|
| Map rendering | [MapLibre Native](https://maplibre.org) via maplibre-compose | BSD-2 |
| Turn-by-turn | [Ferrostar](https://github.com/stadiamaps/ferrostar) | BSD-3 |
| Routing + lane guidance | [Valhalla](https://github.com/valhalla/valhalla) | MIT |
| Map data | OpenStreetMap | ODbL |
| Live traffic | [NDW open data](https://opendata.ndw.nu) (Dutch government, DATEX II) | free, no key |
| Vector tiles | [OpenFreeMap](https://openfreemap.org) (OpenMapTiles schema) | free, no key |
| Places, speed cameras | OpenStreetMap via [Overpass](https://overpass-api.de) | ODbL, no key |
| Typed search | [Photon](https://photon.komoot.io) (OSM geocoder) | Apache-2.0, no key |
| Fuel prices (France) | [prix-carburants](https://www.prix-carburants.gouv.fr) open data | Licence Ouverte, no key |
| Border checks / reported checks (Germany) | [Autobahn GmbH](https://verkehr.autobahn.de/o/autobahn) traffic API (same data as the Autobahn App) | public, no key |

## Layout

- `android/` Kotlin + Jetpack Compose app.
- `style/` Drivr's MapLibre style (`drivr-style.json`, generated by `build_style.py`). Muted palette
  from the prototype; the Android app bundles it as an asset.
- Planned: `tiles/` (Planetiler profile that turns OSM `lanes`/`turn:lanes`/`width` into lane
  polygons, markings and arrows), `style/` (one MapLibre style shared by Android, iOS and web),
  `ios/`.

## Android app

What works today:

- MapLibre map with a driving camera (zoom 17.6, 60° pitch, puck in the lower third).
- Routing through Valhalla with Ferrostar's navigation session: snapping, step advance, rerouting,
  spoken instructions (Android TTS), foreground service.
- Early lane changes: the banner counts the worst-case lane changes to the exit lane and starts
  "Move right now · up to 4 lanes" early enough (400 m per change, at most one left in the final
  500 m, plus a heads-up 1.5 km before that on motorways).
- One layout for everything over the map (banner, a single button column on the right, cards at
  the bottom; a side column in landscape), so nothing overlaps.
  The car always sits in the clear band above the low arrival card; if you pan the map while
  driving, it follows the car again after 10 s.
- Lane guidance banner: maneuver, distance, and a lane strip drawn like the carriageway (dashed
  dividers, painted arrows from OSM `turn:lanes`, lanes to use highlighted).
- Route planner: **Route** on the start screen (or on a place, or a long-press on the map) opens
  From / To fields with a swap button. The start can be your location, any address or place
  (Photon search), a point picked on the map, home, work or a recent destination. Up to three
  routes show on the map with time, distance and "via A28" before anything starts. From your
  location the button says **Start**; from anywhere else it says **Preview drive** and plays the
  trip as a simulation (the phone isn't at the start, so real guidance would reroute at once).
- Start screen: Route, Home, Work and the last destinations as one-tap chips (kept on the phone
  only; change or clear them in Settings › Saved places). The old test-drive card is gone; the
  Utrecht → Amersfoort test drive is under Settings › Developer › Test drive button.
- While driving: a voice on/off button on the map, **Share arrival time** in the ⋯ menu (Android
  share sheet with "On my way to …, arriving around 10:42"), ending a trip asks first (menu or a tap
  on the arrival card), and your speed turns red more than 3 km/h over the limit.
- Settings › Route: avoid tolls, motorways or ferries. Sent to Valhalla as costing options on
  every request, reroutes included (avoid, not forbid: a ferry-only island still gets a route).
- Location from Android's own LocationManager (no Google Play Services).
- Live traffic (Netherlands): accidents, jams, broken-down vehicles and closures from NDW, refreshed
  every 3 minutes. Shown on the map (jams turn the route red), a "Traffic jam in 4.2 km" card for
  the next event on your side of the road (under the turn banner; swipe it away and it stays away
  for that event), and reported delays added to the arrival time.
- Settings (gear on the start screen, or ⋯ while driving): voice, AR, speed cameras, traffic,
  3D buildings, km or miles. AR is off by default.
- Languages: English, Dutch, German, French and Spanish. Drivr follows the phone's language (on
  Android 13+ also the per-app language in system settings) or the one picked in Settings. Menus,
  lane prompts, alerts and errors come from `res/values-xx/strings.xml`; turn instructions and the
  voice come from Valhalla in the same language. To add a language: copy `values/strings.xml` to
  `values-xx/`, translate it, and add the tag to `AppLanguage.SUPPORTED` and
  `res/xml/locales_config.xml`. `StringsCompletenessTest` fails if a key or placeholder is missing.
- AR view (enable in Settings): the camera with the route laid on the road as a solid blue,
  lane-wide carpet, white chevrons flowing along it and a big arrow painted through the turn.
  Opens ~300 m before each maneuver (Auto) or by hand. No ARCore: heading comes from the gyroscope
  (frame rate) corrected by the GPS course, tilt from the gravity sensor, and the phone mount is
  learned from the camera image itself: on straight stretches the road's vanishing point (where
  lane lines meet, found with a small edge-voting estimator in `ar/VanishingPoint.kt`) is straight
  ahead of the car, so its offset from the image centre is how the phone is turned and tilted.
  Between GPS fixes the car is moved along the route at its speed, so the carpet glides.
- Wrong way and off route: raw GPS (not snapped to the route) is checked against the route. Driving
  against it for a few seconds turns the banner (and the AR view) red with a U-turn sign and a
  spoken "turn around"; leaving the route turns it amber with an arrow towards the route.
- Turn list with shields, destinations and lane advice ("Use lanes 4 & 5 of 5").
- Places: search bar and category chips (fuel, charging, food, coffee, groceries, shops, parking,
  rest stops) from OSM. The closest few show within a second or two; while driving the search then
  adds places along the next 40 km of the route (a few small bounding-box queries, raced over
  four Overpass mirrors with Photon as an independent backup, hard 9 s deadline, clear error with
  Retry, last good answer kept as a fallback), in driving
  order with a detour estimate; opening one asks Valhalla for the exact extra time, and **Add
  stop** re-routes via it. Fuel stations show fuel types, hours, shop/toilets; chargers show
  connectors and power.
- Fuel prices where a government publishes them as free, keyless open data. France is wired in.
  The Netherlands and Belgium publish no per-station prices (only CBS national averages), so the
  app says so instead of showing stale crowd prices.
- Route options at the start: up to three ways (Valhalla alternates, ranked with live traffic),
  each with time, distance, "via A2" and its delay.
- Border checks (NL → DE): when a route enters Germany at a crossing with a stationary German
  check (Elten A3, Venlo A40/A61, Bocholtz A4, Gennep A57, Bad Nieuweschans A280, Elmpt A52),
  Drivr also asks Valhalla for the best way around all of them (`exclude_polygons`, 150 m squares,
  which keeps the request under Valhalla's 10 km outline cap) and shows it as an option, e.g.
  through Babberich and Elten village. A delay is only shown when a live source reports one:
  Autobahn GmbH warnings that name a border check, or a jam right at the crossing coming from the
  Netherlands, plus NDW's queue on the Dutch side. Each report shows its source and time; offline
  it says "no live info". No police or Marechaussee feed with check locations exists (their
  presseportal RSS only carries press releases after the fact), so nothing is guessed.
- Settings › Checks: "Always avoid border checks" drives the way around even when slower;
  "Always avoid reported checks" routes around police/traffic checks the Autobahn GmbH reports on
  the route, after a popup that says it can't be a 100% guarantee.
- Jam countdown: inside a jam, a bar shows how far through it you are, metres and minutes left.
- Speed cameras: fixed cameras, red-light cameras and average-speed sections (trajectcontroles)
  from OSM along the route, filtered to your direction. Inside a section it shows your running
  average against the limit.

Build:

```sh
cd android
./gradlew assembleDebug            # APK in app/build/outputs/apk/debug/
./gradlew verifyPaparazziDebug     # unit + screenshot tests
./gradlew recordPaparazziDebug     # re-record screenshots after UI changes
```

CI builds the APK on every push. The latest build of main is always at
[releases/download/latest/drivr.apk](https://github.com/Sarcque/drivr/releases/download/latest/drivr.apk).

### Known limits

- Phone GPS can't tell which lane you're in, so lane advice names the lanes to use rather than
  "move right 1 lane". The AR overlay is accurate to a few metres: good for "turn here", not for
  painting a single lane.
- Routing doesn't avoid jams yet: Valhalla's public server has no live traffic. NDW delays are
  added to the ETA, and closures could be passed to a self-hosted Valhalla as `exclude_polygons`.
- Traffic covers the Netherlands only (NDW). Other countries need their own open feed.
- The map shows OSM roads, not yet the lane-level surface from the web prototype's lane engine
  (`lanegeom.js`). That needs the tile pipeline below.

### Roadmap: what's still missing

Done: lane guidance banner, live NDW traffic with jam countdown, AR junction view, places along
the route with add stop, fuel prices (France), fixed speed cameras and sections.

Next, in order of impact:

1. **Route around jams.** Self-host Valhalla and feed it NDW speeds/closures (`exclude_polygons`,
   or live speeds via Valhalla's traffic tiles). The single biggest gap with Waze/Google.
2. **Offline maps and search.** Bundle PMTiles per region, run Valhalla tiles and a POI index on
   the device. Works in tunnels and abroad, no server costs.
3. **Lane-level map surface.** The Planetiler profile that turns OSM `lanes`/`turn:lanes`/`width`
   into lane polygons and markings (the web prototype's `lanegeom.js`).
4. **Android Auto / Automotive.** Car App Library navigation template, using the same view model.
5. **Driver reports.** Police checks, hazards, objects on the road: needs a small self-hosted
   backend (no open dataset exists for mobile police checks). Crowd fuel prices for NL/BE could
   use the same backend.
6. **EV routing.** Battery model and charging stops (OSM chargers + Valhalla), plus live charger
   availability where open (NDW publishes Dutch charge point status as open data).
7. **More fuel price feeds.** Spain, Italy, Austria (free, keyless); Germany (Tankerkönig, free key).
8. **Voice and UX.** Natural voice per language (Piper TTS, on device), a spoken speeding
   warning, editing and reordering stops in the planner, a light day map style, live location
   sharing (needs a backend; today Share sends the arrival time as text).
9. **Per-country camera rules.** Turn camera warnings off where they're illegal (Germany,
   Switzerland) and show "danger zones" in France.

### Free public servers, and their limits

The app points at public servers so it runs out of the box. Both are free and keyless but are
shared community resources, so don't ship a real release against them:

- Routing: `valhalla1.openstreetmap.de` (FOSSGIS). Fair use, no uptime guarantee. Self-host with
  `docker run -p 8002:8002 -v $PWD/data:/custom_files ghcr.io/valhalla/valhalla-scripted` and an
  OSM extract, then change `VALHALLA_URL` in `android/app/build.gradle.kts`.
- Places and cameras: public Overpass instances (overpass-api.de, private.coffee) and Photon
  (komoot). Same fair-use caveat; both are self-hostable.
- Map tiles: OpenFreeMap, drawn with Drivr's own style. Free with no limits stated; it will be
  joined by Drivr's own lane-level PMTiles bundled on the device.
